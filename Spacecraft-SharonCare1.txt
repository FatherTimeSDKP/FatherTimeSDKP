1 SD&N Geometric Foundation — The Hull IS the Engine
The SharonCare1 propulsion architecture begins not with engineering convention but with geometry. The SD&N (Shape × Dimension × Number) Principle of the FatherTimeSDKP framework establishes that every geometric form encodes its own higher-dimensional states through three intrinsic properties: Faces (F), Vertices (V), and Edges (E). The design of SharonCare1 is the physical instantiation of this principle applied to spacecraft propulsion.
The selection of 12 through-body plasma conduits is not arbitrary. It is the direct application of the dodecahedron — the Platonic solid with F=12 faces — as the governing geometry of the propulsion system. The spherical hull of SharonCare1 is geometrically a dodecahedron, and every structural and propulsive element of the design follows from that single geometric fact.

With 12 faces, the dodecahedral arrangement provides:

2 System Architecture
2.1 Central Distribution Manifold The propulsion system abandons the conventional architecture of external gimbaled engines in favor of a continuous, unified plasma circuit integrated directly into the structural hull. Twelve through-body plasma conduits run the full diameter of the spherical hull, intersecting at the geometric center to form the Central Mixing Chamber — the Plenum.

2.2 The Plenum — Central Mixing Chamber All twelve conduits converge at the geometric center of the spherical hull. This junction serves as the primary distribution hub and self-regulating flow equalizer. By forcing all plasma to transit through this single central chamber, the system ensures pressure equalization and thermal uniformity across the full conduit array before injection into the thrust vectors.
Kinetic turbulence encountered by any single conduit is instantly dampened and redistributed across the remaining manifold via the plenum's pressure-equalization dynamics. This prevents localized hull stress and maintains stable flight characteristics without active intervention from the flight computer — the geometry handles it passively.

3 Magnetic Flux Gating — Vector Control System
Directional authority is achieved through Magnetic Flux Gating at the exit ports of each conduit. This system replaces all conventional mechanical valves, gimbals, and thrust-vectoring actuators with high-fidelity electromagnetic arrays. There are no moving mechanical parts in the propulsion vector control chain.
3.1 Gate Architecture — 20-Node Control Lattice The SD&N encoding places 20 vertex nodes (V=20) at the dodecahedral vertices of the hull. These 20 nodes are the physical locations of the magnetic flux gate arrays — each controlling the plasma flow at the exit ports of adjacent conduits. The V=20 configuration creates a fully connected 4D control state: every gate is within one vertex-edge step of every conduit exit port.

4 Propellant Feed and Storage Architecture
4.1 Conformal Toroidal Tank Configuration Propellant is housed in conformal, toroidal-shaped tanks mounted along the inner radius of the spherical hull's secondary layer. The toroidal geometry is selected specifically for its compatibility with the dodecahedral structural frame: each of the 30 SD&N edge pathways serves as a structural stringer between adjacent toroidal tank segments.
This positioning ensures the center of mass remains constant regardless of propellant depletion level — the toroidal ring empties uniformly from all sectors simultaneously, maintaining the balanced mass distribution required for the gate-modulated attitude control system.
4.2 Cryogenic Injection and Regenerative Cooling High-pressure cryogenic pump system feeds directly into the central Plenum. The pump system provides variable mass-flow rates: low-flow for station-keeping and high-flow for orbital maneuvering and transfer burns
Regenerative cooling loop: propellant circulates around the outer jacket of each conduit before injection, absorbing heat from the conduit wall and pre-heating the fuel. This simultaneously protects the conduit structure and increases thermodynamic efficiency by delivering pre-heated propellant to the Plenum
Plenum pre-conditioning: propellant is thermally and atomically pre-conditioned in the central Plenum before entering the conduit array, ensuring uniform plasma state across all 12 thrust vectors at ignition

The fiber-optic medium is selected specifically to eliminate electromagnetic interference (EMI) from the plasma flow — copper bus architectures are inherently susceptible to EMI-induced command errors in high-plasma environments.

6.2 Dynamic Distribution Algorithm The flight computer implements the Dynamic Distribution Algorithm — a real-time solver that converts incoming navigation commands into gate configuration matrices. The algorithm operates on the SD&N vertex graph of the dodecahedral geometry: each gate node (V=20) is addressed as a node in a 4D control graph, and the solver finds the minimum-energy gate configuration that produces the commanded thrust vector within the angular resolution of 5.81°.
Failover within the algorithm: if a gate reports a fault, the algorithm detects the pressure drop signature within one control cycle (<1 ms) and automatically re-solves the gate configuration matrix using the remaining 11 conduits to maintain the commanded thrust vector with graceful degradation.

7.2 Conduit-as-Chassis Integration All avionics, life support, and payload components are mounted directly to the internal conduit structures, which serve as the primary load-bearing skeleton of the craft. This conduit-as-chassis design eliminates the need for a separate internal frame, maximizing usable volume. Equipment mounting points utilize active piezoelectric dampers to neutralize micro-vibrations generated by plasma flow.
8 Redundancy and Fail-Safe Protocols
8.1 Graceful Degradation — N+1 Architecture The central manifold design provides inherent N+1 redundancy without additional hardware. If any single conduit suffers a blockage, gate failure, or structural compromise, the central Plenum automatically re-routes plasma to the remaining 11 conduits. The Dynamic Distribution Algorithm detects the pressure drop signature within <1 ms and re-solves the thrust vector
11/12 conduits: Graceful degradation — 2,048 discrete states — full mission capability
10/12 conduits: Reduced vectoring — 1,024 states — primary mission with constraints
9/12 conduits: Minimum viable — 512 states — emergency return/station-keeping only
<9/12 conduits: Emergency protocol — passive attitude control — emergency beacon active
Fail-closed design: power loss → maximum magnetic pinch on ALL gates → full shutdown
No conduit failure can produce an uncontrolled thrust event.
8.2 Emergency Protocols Fail-closed emergency shutdown: All magnetic flux gates are designed as fail-closed. If primary power is lost, the magnetic field defaults to maximum pinch state on all 12 exit ports. Plasma flow stops within microseconds. No mechanical actuator failure mode can produce uncontrolled thrust
Structural safety factor: Spherical hull rated for 1.5× maximum operating Plenum pressure. Each conduit is double-walled with vacuum gap between the primary plasma bore and the external hull wall
Emergency Plenum venting: Passive secondary venting system depressurizes the central Plenum into deep space within 10 seconds if internal pressure exceeds safe operating limits
Thermal runaway protection: Redundant thermocouple arrays in each conduit wall segment report to the flight computer at 1 kHz. Automatic gate closure initiated if conduit wall temperature approaches W-Re safety threshold
