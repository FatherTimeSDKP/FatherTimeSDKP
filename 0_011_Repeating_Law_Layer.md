0.011 Repeating Law Layer

1. Purpose

The 0.011 repeating concept describes a numerical residual relationship observed when dividing a complete quantity into three equal parts and recombining the rounded decimal representations.

The purpose of this layer is to study:

* repeating decimal behavior,
* finite precision effects,
* cyclic numerical representations,
* relationships between division, reconstruction, and mathematical constants.

⸻

2. Equal Division of a Whole

A complete unit divided into three equal sections is:

[
\frac{1}{3}=0.333333\ldots
]

Adding three identical sections:

[
0.333333\ldots+
0.333333\ldots+
0.333333\ldots
]

gives:

[
0.999999\ldots
]

In standard real-number mathematics:

[
0.999999\ldots=1
]

because the difference between them is:

[
1-0.999999\ldots=0
]

⸻

3. Finite Precision Residual

When the repeating decimal is truncated:

[
0.3333333333
]

rather than using the infinite series:

[
0.333333\ldots
]

the reconstruction produces:

[
3(0.3333333333)=0.9999999999
]

The remaining difference is:

[
1-0.9999999999=0.0000000001
]

This is a precision residual.

⸻

4. Definition of the 0.011 Repeating Observation

The proposed residual form:

[
0.011111\ldots
]

represents the difference introduced when a repeating decimal approximation is compared against a reconstructed whole.

The general form:

[
R_n=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i
]

where:

* (R_n) = residual after finite representation
* (a_i) = decimal approximation terms

As (n) increases:

[
R_n\rightarrow0
]

⸻

5. Relationship to Circular Measurement

The circle constant:

[
\pi
]

is defined as:

[
\pi=\frac{C}{d}
]

where:

* (C) = circumference
* (d) = diameter

Because (\pi) is irrational:

[
\pi=3.1415926535\ldots
]

its decimal expansion does not terminate or repeat.

The 0.011 repeating observation investigates whether finite decimal reconstruction creates measurable numerical structures when circular relationships are approximated.
